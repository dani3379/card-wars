# Cards arrive like objects, not like panels
**2026-09-10**

## The diagnosis

The shell's motion kit says it plainly in its own header:

> `deal_in` / `hover_lift` / `claim_pop` animate **modulate + scale ONLY — never
> position or size** — so container layout is untouched.

The constraint is real: these tiles live in containers, and a container owns its
children's `position`. But its consequence had never been priced:

**Alpha and scale are the two properties a physical object never changes.**

A card does not fade up from nothing and it does not grow from 60%. It TRAVELS,
it TURNS OVER, and it LANDS. Fading is the idiom for *a UI element appeared*;
every card game worth copying uses the idiom for *an object arrived*. That is the
whole of why the slates read "a bit weird" — the motion was correct for a dialog
box and applied to a deck of cards.

Note the one place that was already right: **the combat hand.** `set_hand_target`
springs the card's POSITION, tweens its rotation, and staggers the deal. It is
the only card surface in the game that was not built out of the shell kit, and it
is the only one that already felt like a deal.

## The fix — `Motion.deal_card`

The container constraint is honoured rather than broken. A tile in a container
owns its `position`; its CHILD does not — and every card slate here already
builds exactly that shape (`card_wrap` sized by the row, holding the Card2D). So
the wrapper stays put and the FACE flies.

Four things carry it:

- **TRAVEL along a bowed path.** A dealt card arcs; it does not lerp. The bow is
  perpendicular to the flight and a FRACTION of its length, so a short hop and a
  long throw curve alike.
- **A TURN.** `scale.x` runs from foreshortened to full, so the card is turning
  face-up as it lands. `Motion.turn_face` already existed for exactly this and
  had one caller.
- **A SHADOW THAT KNOWS THE HEIGHT.** Wide, pale and thrown clear while the card
  is in the air; tight, dark and under it on contact. This is the cheapest 3D cue
  in 2D and nothing in the kit was doing it.
- **A LANDING.** Overshoot, a contact squash snapped on with no travel time (a
  squash you can watch arrive is a wobble), and the rotation settling past zero
  and back.

And **no alpha tween anywhere in it.** A card is opaque.

## Two rules that fell out of it

**A card is dealt; a price tag is UI.** The Shop faded card and buy-button
together as one slot, which is what made the card behave like a panel. They are
not the same kind of thing — a card is an OBJECT and gets thrown onto the
counter, a price is a LABEL and may honestly fade in behind it. Splitting the two
is most of what makes the shelf read as stock rather than as a form. Same split
on the draft slate: the card is dealt, PICK fades.

**The shadow has to be under its object.** The first cut drew it at the wrapper's
fixed foot, so it sat at the landing spot while the card was still across the
screen — that is not a shadow, it is a target marker, and the card read as
floating free of the table. It tracks the card now, DAMPED and unequally per
axis: a light roughly overhead puts the shadow nearly under the card
horizontally but much less displaced vertically, because vertical screen distance
on a flat-laid card is mostly depth, not height. Tracking 1:1 welds the shadow to
the card and kills the separation the cue exists for.

## The hover, for free

Once height is a channel, the hover can use it. `hover_lift` was scale 1.03 — "this
is bigger". Driving the same `deal_lift` to 0.30 on hover makes the shadow spread
and pale at the same moment, which says "this has come OFF THE TABLE". That is
what the player is actually being told, and it is what makes the scale read as a
lift rather than a zoom.

## Items are SET DOWN, not dealt — `Motion.drop_item`

Same machinery, different physics, because they are different events. A card is
thrown across a table by a dealer: it travels far, arcs, and TURNS OVER because
it left the hand face-down. A relic is spoils being put on the table in front of
you — it drops a short way, straight, already face-up, and lands heavier. Giving
the spoils row the card's deal made three plaques skate in sideways like playing
cards, which is the wrong story.

So `drop_item` is a short steep fall, almost no bow, a fraction of the spin, and
**no turn at all** — foreshortening a framed plaque just squashes it.

**Treasure inverts the throw**, and the staging demands it: the chest is open
directly below the tile, so the find RISES out of the box rather than falling
from the sky onto an open chest.

## The container-child path

Relic tiles live directly in an HBox, not in a `card_wrap`. So the flight is
relative to **wherever the face rests**, not to zero, and the rest position is
read at TAKE-OFF rather than at arm time — a screen that equalises tile heights
(Reward does, by writing `custom_minimum_size` after the row is built) re-sorts
its container on the following frame, so a position captured at arm time is
already stale.

**The bug this path produced, and how it was caught:** the face was held
`visible = false` through its stagger delay. **A container skips hidden children
when it sorts**, so the tile was never given a slot, `rest_hold` read the
unsorted (0,0), and both relic tiles landed stacked on the row's left edge. It is
`modulate.a` now — a hard on/off with no tween, so it is not the alpha ramp this
verb exists to remove; the card is absent, and then it is thrown.

Only the SETTLED shot could catch that. A mid-flight frame looks identical either
way, which is exactly why the harness boots the real screens as its second phase.

## Wired

| surface | object | companion |
|---|---|---|
| Recruit | card dealt, alternating sides, accelerating stagger | — |
| Shop | card dealt onto the counter | buy button fades after landing |
| NetDraft | card dealt, alternating sides | PICK fades after landing |
| Reward | relic tiles dropped onto the table | — |
| Treasure | relic rises out of the chest | — |

The three card slates gained a height-aware contact shadow. Recruit's replaced
three stacked `draw_circle` passes "to fake the blur" — the hand-rolled-gradient
defect, and a shadow of FIXED size under a card that now flies.

The item rows get no shadow: a tile that moves cannot draw its own contact
shadow (it would carry it), and the row would have to draw for all of them. The
drop and its landing squash carry the weight on their own.

## Harness

`tools/_shot_card_deal.tscn`. It **stages its own card** rather than booting a
screen, and that is the whole reason it works. Two earlier cuts tried to
photograph the live Recruit slate:

1. **sampling a real clock** — one capture (`frame_post_draw` + `get_image()` + a
   PNG encode) costs ~0.9s of wall time, so the first frame landed at t=1.02 for
   a shot requested at t=0.10 and the deal was long over.
2. **slowing `Engine.time_scale`** — ten back-to-back captures then advanced the
   animation by 0.17s in total, which does not even reach the deal's start delay.

Both were measuring the machine. Driving the verb directly with a deliberately
long flight makes the sampling rate irrelevant, and the frames are of a KNOWN
parameter value rather than of whenever the harness got scheduled. A second phase
then boots the real screens and shoots them SETTLED, because a flight that ends
anywhere but exactly (0,0) leaves every card permanently offset in its slot — and
that is invisible in a mid-flight frame.

Three traps it cost:
- `CardDB.CARD_POOL` is a const **Dictionary**, so `CARD_POOL[0]` is folded at
  compile time and takes the whole script down as a parse error.
- The accessor is `get_card_data`, not `get_card`; the failed call **aborted
  `_ready` silently** and the harness sat there with an open window and no output.
- `weakref()` is declared Variant, so `var x := weakref(n)` infers Variant and
  this project promotes that warning to an error. Type it `WeakRef`.

## The Collection — how cards LOAD, which is a different question

The gallery is virtualised: a ghost writ stands in each slot, the real Card2D
bakes on a worker thread, and the swap was a **hard cut** — `add_child` next to
`queue_free`, one frame, no transition. Scrolling the collection was a field of
cards snapping into existence, and that is the most literal answer there is to
"the way cards load is a bit weird".

**It is NOT a cross-fade, and that needed arguing** — it is the one place in this
pass where fading would have been defensible. Everywhere else the rule is that
objects do not change alpha. But here the object does not ARRIVE: the ghost and
the card are the same card in the same slot (identical 225×300 tile, so nothing
even reflows), and what changes is that it becomes legible. Blurring between two
states of one thing says *loading*; the fiction says the gold leaf just went on.

So the card lands at full strength and a **specular sweeps it**, tinted by
rarity — a positive event rather than a soft blur, which is the same
snap-and-punctuate as the rest of the pass. A rare resolving out of the ghosts
announces itself in the gallery exactly as it does on a reward slate.

Verified by consecutive-frame diff rather than a settled shot: a 0.34s sweep is
over long before a screen settles, so the harness takes frames from the moment
the grid starts filling and looks for moving bright bands.

## Not done

- **Dealing the Collection grid** is deliberately not done: dealing 200 cards is
  not a deal, it is a hailstorm. The hydration beat is the right scale there.
- **Gallery hover** — Collection still has no hover feedback at all.
- **The potion shelf** in Shop and the Event screens' choice tiles still use
  `deal_in`. A potion bottle is arguably an object like a relic; an event choice
  is genuinely a UI panel and should keep fading.
