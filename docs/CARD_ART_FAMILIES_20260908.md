# CARD ART — THE SIX FAMILIES (2026-09-08)

The direction: **one universe, six registers.** Cards vary in tone — cute beside terrifying —
while everything that makes them the same world stays locked. This resolves the tension in
[`CARD_ART_LIFE_PASS_20260908.md`](CARD_ART_LIFE_PASS_20260908.md) §0, where the six "cute"
cards turned out to be the only ones with any warmth in them.

## 2. The six registers — each derived from the tribe's own mechanic

The rule that makes this a system and not a mess: **the register comes from what the tribe
does.** Then the art teaches the rules, and the board becomes readable at a glance — which is
the standing complaint in `feedback_combat_feels_samey` (presentation and legibility, not
mechanics).

| tribe | n | mechanic | register | the note |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Soldier** | 39 | ENDURE — line, formation | **Human. Worn. Gallows-funny.** | The anchor everything else deviates from. Tired, muddy, complaining. Not cute, not horrific — *people*. This is the "heroic" leg. **Faces visible.** |
| **Beast** | 31 | ARRIVE — on-enter | **Charming-feral.** | Where CUTE lives, fully licensed. Big eyes, wet nose, and it takes your hand off. Card Wars energy. Cute that can hurt you. |
| **Dead** | 33 | DIE — the death spine | **Melancholy-comic.** | NOT horror — that is the standing tone rule. The dead are sad and faintly absurd: a skeleton carrying its own arm, a ghost who has forgotten why it came. Over the Garden Wall, not Resident Evil. |
| **Warlock** | 15 | SPELLCRAFT | **Genuinely unsettling.** | Where TERRIFYING lives — and only here. Wrong anatomy, one finger too many, a smile a little too wide. |
| **Bastion** | 14 | SIEGEWORKS — walls | **Monumental deadpan.** | The comedy of scale. Enormous dumb heavy objects with one small worried man on them. Give every wall a garrison. |
| **Stray** | 18 | MASTERLESS — larceny | **Sly. Caught. Fourth-wall.** | The tribe that looks at you. Mid-theft, half-turned, unrepentant. The wink lives here — and so does the cat. |

**Why the horror is rationed to 15 cards.** Warlock is your smallest tribe, and that is the
feature. Your tone rule says grim/funny/heroic, **not horror** — and a game that is uniformly
frightening isn't frightening. Confining the dread to 15 of 167 cards means that when a Warlock
lands, the temperature actually drops. Terror is a *seasoning*, and the tribe roster hands you
the dose already measured.

## 3. What locks the universe — the part that must not vary

This is the whole discipline. **Vary one axis, lock the rest.** If register varies and anything
else drifts, "six families" degrades into "inconsistent art" — which is the failure mode and the
only real risk in this direction.

Locked across all six, no exceptions:

1. **The writ.** Every card is a document of the same war — the v9 illuminated-writ frame is
   already the strongest unifier you own, and it does this work for free. A horrifying thing and
   an adorable thing filed on the same parchment *are* the same world by filing.
2. **One light.** Firelit, warm, single source, chiaroscuro. Even the horror is lit by the same
   campfire as the cat.
3. **One palette.** Sepia, ochre, umber, ember. **No register gets its own hue** — the Warlocks
   do not go cold-blue and the Beasts do not go pastel. Dread comes from *what is drawn*, never
   from a colour shift. (This is also what keeps the cyan problem from coming back through the
   front door.)
4. **One material.** Hand-painted, visible brushwork, ink linework. No family switches medium —
   no cel-shading for the cute ones, no photoreal for the scary ones.
5. **Shared circumstance — the real binder.** *Everything is on the same road.* The adorable
   beast is muddy. The horror is standing in a rut. The wall has laundry on it. Cult of the Lamb
   and Don't Starve hold together not through drawing style but through world logic, and so does
   this: they are all in the same war, in the same weather, having the same bad month.

Registers 1–4 are already true of most of the pool. **Number 5 is the one to add**, and it is a
single clause in a prompt.

## 4. Devices for putting cute and terrifying in the same frame

The register table gives you variety *across* cards. These give you the collision *inside* one.

**① The Doom turn — free, and already mechanically yours.** A cheerful little thing with a
countdown to detonation. Nine cards. Draw them *adorable and unmistakably doomed* — the sprite
grinning up at you while the fuse it is sitting on gets shorter. The card's own mechanic supplies
the horror; the art only has to supply the charm. This is the highest-value single idea here.

**② Cute form, terrible act.** `the_glutton` already does it — caught mid-meal, looking up at
you. The camp cat with something it should not have in its mouth. The charm is what makes the
act land.

**③ Scale mismatch.** A tiny endearing thing casting an enormous wrong shadow. Cheap, and it
reads at thumbnail size.

**④ Let the Dead be funny and the Warlocks never be.** The two neighbouring registers must not
blur. Dead is where you are allowed a joke about mortality; Warlock is where nobody is joking.
Keeping that line sharp is what makes both work.

**⑤ The "+" forge as a turn** — a forged card repainted as *the same creature, but something has
changed about it*. Powerful, but it doubles the art bill; reserve it for a handful of signature
cards rather than the pool.

4. **Add the shared-circumstance clause** (§3.5) to every prompt going forward. Free.
5. **Then** the Soldier de-helming pass from the LIFE doc — 39 cards, the largest block, and the
   anchor register everything else is measured against.

## 6. The prompt shape

Take the revised tail from
[`CARD_ART_LIFE_PASS_20260908.md`](CARD_ART_LIFE_PASS_20260908.md) §6 and insert **one register
clause** per tribe, immediately after the subject. Everything after that clause stays byte-identical
across all six families — that is what enforces §3 mechanically rather than by good intentions.

```
[SUBJECT, caught mid-action, one thing broken or worn wrong], [REGISTER CLAUSE], mud and kit of the same war on it,
  <-- then the locked tail, identical for every tribe -->
grim-storybook dark-fantasy character, hand-painted in the Darkest Dungeon style of Chris Bourassa ... --ar 3:2 --v 8.1 --s 400 --no cyan, teal, ...
```

Register clauses:

- **Soldier** — `a weary human face, unhelmed, gallows humour in the expression`
- **Beast** — `endearing and clearly dangerous, expressive animal eyes`
- **Dead** — `melancholy and faintly absurd, dignity slipping`
- **Warlock** — `quietly wrong, anatomy subtly incorrect, unsettling calm`
- **Bastion** — `immense and deadpan, one small human for scale`
- **Stray** — `sly, caught in the act, looking straight at the viewer`

`--sref` still does the cohesion work across the batch. The register clause is deliberately the
*only* variable — it is early in the prompt where it carries weight, and everything downstream of
it is locked.
